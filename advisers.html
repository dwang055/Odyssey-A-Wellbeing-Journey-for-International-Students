Guide for advisers and teachers

How to use Odyssey with international students in orientation, pastoral or language classes, and what to watch for.

What Odyssey is

Three short chapters, about ten minutes each, in English and Chinese. It works offline, can be installed to a phone's home screen, and collects no personal information (see Privacy and accessibility).

  • Where am I? Naming the strengths students brought from home across the four walls of Te Whare Tapa Whā, then putting down new roots in the whenua (land and belonging) here.
  • Who am I? Answering harsh thoughts with balanced ones, plus a self-compassion exercise. The thoughts cover the stressors Chinese international students report most: language, isolation, discrimination, academic pressure and guilt toward family. Each wall draws one of two thoughts, so replays differ.
  • Who are they? Belonging, pulling away, and small steps back towards people, with a ready-made opening line for each step.

A small traveller carries the story. It starts with a dim lantern and a grey cloud overhead. Each finished chapter brings colour back to part of the island, and the traveller heals a little: the cloud lifts, the lantern brightens, and it stands taller. On the main screen, students can tap any place on the island to read what happens there, the ideas behind it and why it helps, before they play.

The content validates before it corrects: each harsh thought is first acknowledged as understandable, and discrimination is named as unfair treatment, never as a thinking error.

Each chapter ends with a skill card and one small step for the week, with a time attached. Students can copy their plan to send to a friend. Nothing they type leaves their device.

What it is not

Odyssey is a learning prototype. It is not therapy, screening or diagnosis, and it is not suitable for a student who is in crisis. It collects no data, so it cannot alert you to anything a student enters.

A 45-minute group session

  1. 0–5Open by normalising it: settling into a new country is hard for almost everyone, and the hardest point can come months in, not in week one. Show the "Need to talk?" button.
  2. 5–15Pairs play Chapter 1 together on one phone or laptop, then compare toolkits.
  3. 15–25Pairs choose Chapter 2 or Chapter 3. Pairs work better than solo play.
  4. 25–35Pairs use the "Playing with a friend?" prompt at the end of their chapter.
  5. 35–42Open "How help works here" on the main screen together. Show where your campus counselling and health services are, and how to book.
  6. 42–45Each student copies their plan to a friend, a family member or themselves.

Why pairs and follow-up matter

Chinese international students in one New Zealand study preferred to turn to parents and friends rather than student services, and few had used counselling despite high distress. Reviews of digital mental health tools also find that light human support, such as encouragement and checking in on goals, often improves how much people use them and how much they get from them. Peers can provide this, not only clinicians.

A simple follow-up works: a week later, ask the group "Did anyone try their step?" Keep it optional and never single anyone out.

Set it up for your campus

Open Campus setup and enter your counselling, urgent, health centre and international support details. It produces a campus-config.js file that you place in the Odyssey folder. Your services then appear in every support panel, alongside the national helplines.

Odyssey can sit within your wellbeing work under the Pastoral Care Code of Practice 2021, which asks providers to support learners' wellbeing and gives international learners their own outcomes. It is one small tool, not a substitute for those services.

Safety

  • Never ask students to share their private reflections aloud. The reflection boxes are private by design.
  • If a student seems distressed, or says the session left them feeling heavier, check in privately and follow your institution's pastoral care and critical incident procedures.
  • Know your local referral path before you run a session: campus counselling, health centre, and the international student team.

Before wider use

  • Ask your counselling service to review the balanced replies and safety wording.
  • Ask Māori advisers to review how Te Whare Tapa Whā is presented in Chapter 1.
  • Confirm the helpline details and your campus service details are current.
  • If you plan to evaluate it formally, seek ethics approval and pair the app with a separate anonymous survey.
